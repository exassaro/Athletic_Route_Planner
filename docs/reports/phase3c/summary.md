## Fixed ends and Case B (step 10)

A run whose end is fixed, or a Case B run that ends at its target, gets one through via on its longest leg. Candidates sit on ellipses around the leg's two ends and are ranked on the walking matrix. The best are routed and measured, and the via moves along its ray until the run is within 0.5% of the target (at least 50 m). Rays are searched in parallel.

## Loops

**Without stops.** Three vias lie on a circle or an oval through the start, with a perimeter of D / 1.3, leaning towards the requested direction. Without a direction there are six rotations plus two ovals. GraphHopper routes each shape, and the shape is rescaled on the measured length until it is within min(500 m, 3% of D). Fixes found on the live network:
- a via in the sea or a river is pulled towards the centre;
- a via that makes a spur is moved;
- a via on a dead-end stub is moved to where the route left the road;
- the start's own "lollipop" stem is not counted as doubling back.

**With stops.** The stops are ordered as a closed tour on the walking matrix and planned like any run (pass near, reach, bridge, road). The tour is routed back to the start, and one leg is lengthened: the longest first, then the others (amendment 24). If every stop is passed at the start itself, an ordinary loop is made. If the tour alone exceeds D + 500 m, the result is `over_budget` with the full loop. A stop that cannot be reached and left within D gives a `loop_stop_too_far` conflict.

## Choices made during the phase (say if any should change)

1. **The Case B detour is offered only if it is spur-free.** It is an extra option, so a detour with a dead-end excursion is left out rather than shown. A fixed end keeps its best detour and flags any spur, because the user asked for that end.
2. **Lengthening ranks within-tolerance attempts first.** A clean attempt outside ±500 m no longer beats one inside it that has a flagged spur. F02's "shortest" regressed in step 2 for this reason and was fixed here.
3. **Loop vias snap within 400 m** (point-to-point via candidates: 150 m). A loop via only shapes the route, and 150 m discarded most shapes in rural Kerala.
4. **The second loop must meet the 3% goal.** A loop between 3% of D and 500 m is offered only when no loop reaches the goal (amendment 22).
5. **The out-and-back fallback follows the blueprint's "fewer than two shapes survive" rule.** When only one loop shape passes the hard rules, the result offers that loop and an out-and-back (LX01, LX02, LX04, LX09). When two shapes pass but only one meets the 3% goal, the result is a single loop without the fallback (LX08).
6. **Loop stop gate for passed-near places.** It uses twice (the way there − 1 km), since the loop may turn up to 1 km before the place. A place the loop must reach uses twice the way there.

## Findings worth knowing

- **Lean is approximate.** The bearing from the start to a loop's centre is 1° to 36° off the requested direction in most fixtures, but 69° for LX02 (Ernakulam, north), where backwaters cut off the western shapes. Section 16 has no lean KPI, so this is reported only.
- **The network is jumpy.** Moving a via a little can change the route length by kilometres (a new bridge or road). Bracketing and secant steps handle most cases; a few shapes give up after 2 or 3 tries.
- **Hills and coasts produce out-and-backs.** Kalpetta (LX10) and Chellanam (S14) have no loop at 8 to 10 km by these shapes. Per-area circuity learning and other shapes are Phase 7 work.
- **Harness method change.** Pedestrian map-matching sometimes skips a walkable stretch. LX09 is an example: 77 m on a sand service road at Muzhappilangad beach, tagged `foot=yes`. The harness now locates each skipped stretch with pedestrian costing and checks that way's OSM tags, like every matched way. Before, any skip over 1% / 30 m counted as an access violation.
- **Latency headroom.** Point-to-point p95 is about 4 s against the 5 s target. The slowest fixtures are Case B runs, which search three options per alternative.
